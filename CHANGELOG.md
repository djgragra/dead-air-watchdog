# Changelog

## 26.10.12 — 2026-10-30

- **Menu: "Window → Zoom" removed on Windows and Linux.** It did nothing there (it is a macOS command); the zoom of the page stays in the *View* menu (Ctrl +, Ctrl −, Ctrl 0) and now also works with Ctrl and the mouse wheel. Thanks to Paul, who found it and suggested the wheel.
- **Menu bar hidden in the compact view (Windows and Linux).** *Settings → General → Display → Hide the menu bar in the compact view*, or an item in the tray menu. Right-click the compact window to bring it back (the menu also has the views, always on top and the zoom), or press Alt for a moment. Thanks to Paul, who suggested it.
- **Wall display: names up to "Very large"** (*Settings → General → Display → Names in the wall display*), so the name of the input that went silent can be read from far away. Thanks to Paul and a colleague of his.
- **Compact view, narrow window.** The buttons stay on the first line and the status badge is the one that goes to the next line; in a very narrow window (about 250 px) the level bars give way, so the names are not cut. Thanks to Paul.
- **Compact view, shorter window.** When the window gets shorter while the list is at its end, the end of the list stays in sight and the top rows go first. Thanks to Paul. Not tried on a real Windows PC.
- **Wall display: logos of "Medium" size**, between the small one next to the name and the large one.
- Tests: new ones for the menu without "Zoom", the wheel zoom, the hidden menu bar, the right-click menu, the new sizes and the narrow compact layout (`dev/window-menu.test.js`). The menus and the window were not run on a real Windows PC.

## 26.10.11 — 2026-10-26

- **Micro-silences can be switched off.** *Settings → General → Write micro-silences in the event log* switches them off for all the inputs, and each input can decide for itself in *More options → Micro-silences in the log* (*Same as in General*, *Write them*, *Do not write them*). With spoken-word channels, or music without crossfades, they could fill the log. An input that has them off writes no micro-silence, shows no counter on its tile, and the counter and the filter of the Dashboard and of the log disappear when all the inputs have them off (the micro-silences already in the log stay until they expire). They are on by default, so nothing changes for you unless you switch them off. The alarm, the alerts and the yellow light are not affected; the summary cannot use the micro-silences of an input that has them off to tell a fault of the stream from one of the network.
- **Compact view and always on top.** A button at the bottom of the side bar (and an item in the tray menu) turns the window into a small one for a screen full of other programs: one row per input with its name, its state and two thin level meters, no side bar, a smallest window of about 280 × 110 pixels. *Normal view* brings the window back. *Always on top* keeps the window above the others in either view. Both choices and the place and size of each view are remembered; nothing changes in the detection or in the alerts. Thanks to Paul, who suggested it.
- Tests: 278 (17 new: micro-silences off for all, for one input, and while running; the compact view and its sizes; what a backup leaves on this PC). The compact view and the pin were run in the real window on Linux only. Not tried: real audio, the installed app, and the compact view on Windows and macOS (see the README).

## 26.10.10 — 2026-10-22

- **Several sound cards at once.** Settings → Inputs → *Add sound card* can now be used as many times as you need, up to the 32 inputs of the app, mixed with streams as you like. Each sound-card input has its own device and its own pair of channels (1–2, 3–4, 5–6, 7–8), so you can monitor several cards, or several pairs of one card (Dante Virtual Soundcard, an AoIP driver, Virtual Audio Cable lines). To tell identical cards apart (two "USB PnP Sound Device"), they are numbered in the list; a device already used by another input is marked in the list; each input in Settings shows its level while monitoring runs, so playing something into one card shows which input it is; and the device name is the tooltip of "Sound card" on the tile. A saved device that is no longer there is shown as "not found" and reported as a problem: the input never moves to another card by itself. Settings saved by earlier versions are unchanged. Not tried with real hardware: opening many cards together, and a card in exclusive use by another program on Windows (the input reports it). Thanks to Paul and Richard, who asked for it.
- **Your own alert wording.** Settings → Alerts → *Wording of the alerts*: for each message (dead air, frozen audio, reminder, audio back, several inputs down, several inputs back) you can write the subject and the text, using `{input}`, `{cause}`, `{start}`, `{end}`, `{now}`, `{threshold}`, `{seconds}`, `{tolerance}`, `{duration}`, `{host}` (and `{n}`, `{list}` for the grouped messages). An empty field keeps the text of the app. The subject always starts with `[Dead Air Watchdog · installation]`. Email, Telegram and the HTTP alert use it. Thanks to Paul, who suggested it.
- **Fix: Save disappeared in Settings.** In a wide window the *Save settings* bar went behind the top bar when the page was scrolled. It now stays visible under the top bar. Thanks to Paul, who reported it.
- **Fix: the menu bar was missing after the wall display.** F11 (full screen) followed by *Wall display* and *Exit wall display* left the window without its menu bar on Windows. The menu bar is now restored when full screen ends. Not tried on a real Windows PC. Thanks to Paul, who reported it.
- Tests: 259 (16 new: the alert wording, eight cards and several pairs in the real engine against a stub, the store and the audio windows with several cards, the link of the update alert). Not tried: real audio and the installed app (see the README).

## 26.10.9 — 2026-10-21

- **Fix: macOS on Apple Silicon said the app was "damaged".** The Apple Silicon build had a signature that was no longer valid (macOS: "code has no resources but signature indicates they must be present"), and recent macOS versions refuse such an app instead of offering to open it. The app is now signed ad hoc during the build (no Apple account is needed) and the build checks the signature of the app inside each disk image. The first start still shows the usual "unidentified developer" notice: *System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway*. If you already downloaded an earlier version and it will not open, run in Terminal: `xattr -cr "/Applications/Dead Air Watchdog.app" && codesign --force --deep -s - "/Applications/Dead Air Watchdog.app"`. Thanks to Rob, who reported it.
- **HTTP alerts (webhook).** Settings → Alerts → HTTP: for every alarm, reminder and recovery the app calls an address you choose, so a studio screen, Node-RED, Home Assistant or a status page can react. POST sends JSON (`event`, `time`, `installation`, `host`, `message` and a list of `inputs` with `name`, `cause`, `since`, `until`, `durationSec`); GET puts the same facts in the address. Optional `Authorization` header. Off by default, with a *Send test* button, the same retries as email and the same grouping when several inputs fail together. The address and the header are secrets: stored encrypted, left out of the backup unless you include passwords and tokens, and never shown in the log or in errors. Thanks to Gavin, who suggested it.
- **Fix: a stream that is slow to start was restarted for ever.** The app gave a stream 3 s to deliver its first audio; a server that needs 5 to 10 s (a 128 kbit/s stream took 6.6 s to start) was disconnected and retried again and again, with the traffic light turning yellow and then red although the stream played fine in a browser. Now a stream gets 20 s to start; once it has played, a stop of 3 s is still reported at once. A stream that never starts is reported as "the stream did not start (no audio after 20 s)". Thanks to Paul, who reported it.
- Tests: 243 (11 new: HTTP alerts against a real local server, the whole chain from a silent stream to the request, retries, secrets; a slow-starting stream and a stalled one; the ad-hoc signature hook).

## 26.10.8 — 2026-10-19

- **Yellow after…** Settings → Inputs → *More options*: how long the audio must stay below the threshold before the traffic light of the input turns yellow (2 s by default, 0 = as before). With speech the pauses between words no longer flash yellow, above all on the wall display with many inputs. Only the colour changes: the red alarm still counts from the first quiet moment, and an input that delivers no data (stream down) turns yellow at once. Thanks to Richard, who suggested it.
- **Fix: a false alarm during speech.** Audio counted as "back" only after 2 s above the threshold *in a row*; speech with pauses below the threshold more often than every 2 s never reached that, so the countdown ran on and an alarm could go off after the alert time while somebody was talking. Now audio is back after 2 s in a row or 2 s in total within 5 s (same rule to end an alarm). Found while testing the yellow delay.
- **Monitoring hours** (per input, *More options*): days and hours in which the input is **not monitored**, or has **its own threshold and time before the alarm**; windows may go past midnight (they belong to the day they start on). While off, the input is measured and shown as PAUSED until the end of the window, nothing is judged, sent or sounded; an alarm that is open when the hours begin is closed in the log (note "Closed by the monitoring hours") without a "back" message; when they end the countdown starts again.
- **Frozen-audio alarm** (per input, off by default): the level stays within 1 dB (adjustable) for the time you set, at least 10 s, although it is above the threshold: a steady tone, a stuck loop, constant noise. Own cause "Frozen audio" in the log, the alerts and the desktop notification; it ends, with a "back" message, when the level moves again, or quietly when the audio goes away (then the silence alarm takes over).
- **Summary** (Event log → Summary): availability per input (share of the monitored time without dead air; the time the app was not running, pauses and hours switched off do not count), alarms, total and longest dead air, micro-silences; this month, last month, last 7 or 30 days; **Export summary CSV**.
- **Common problems.** When at least 3 inputs (2 if there are fewer, or 30% of them if that is lower) lose their data or go into alarm within 10 s of each other, the events are marked "Common problem" in the log and listed under the summary, with the availability also given without them: probably the network or the computer that monitors, not the stream servers. The event log CSV has a new column `common_problem`. Frozen audio and plain silence are never counted as common.
- **Wall display: "ALL OK" much larger.** The short status (ALL OK / PAUSED / STOPPED) grows to fill the width the clock leaves, up to the height of the clock; on a 4:3 screen it goes on a line of its own under the clock. The long "DEAD AIR · name" line is as before.
- **Italian: "Dashboard" and "Wall display"** stay in English (they were "Cruscotto" and "Modalità parete" / "schermo a muro"). Spanish unchanged.
- **The tip jar in Help & about is a link too**: a click on the jar does what the Tip jar button does (hidden from keyboard and screen readers; the button stays the accessible control).
- Dependencies: `http-cache-semantics` 4.2.0 → 4.3.0 (patch, build tooling only; clears the one "high" audit advisory).
- Tests: 232 (45 new: speech and yellow delay in the detector, monitoring hours, frozen audio, availability and common problems, the settings limits, Italian terms, the window wiring, the jar). Not tried: real audio and the installed app (see the README).

## 26.10.7 — 2026-10-17

- **Alerts by email no longer go to the sender.** The message used the sender's own address in "To" with the real recipients in Bcc, so the mailbox of the sender received a copy of every alert (two mailboxes read by the same person meant every alert twice). "To" is now `undisclosed-recipients:;` and only the real recipients are in the message envelope. A sender that is also a recipient still gets one copy.
- **Tip card** in Help & about, at the bottom: the tip jar of the site, one line and a *Tip jar* button that opens the site page in your language. No pop-up, no reminder, nothing in the main window.
- **Shared `updater` module** unchanged (version 2). Dependencies updated: Electron 44.9.0, Nodemailer 10.1.2.
- Tests: 185 (4 new: the envelope with the real Nodemailer, a sender that is also a recipient, the tip card texts, link and packaging).

## 26.10.6 — 2026-10-15

- **New logo and new look.** The logo is drawn as SVG (`assets/logo.svg`, `assets/tray.svg`): level bars fading to a flat line above the three lamps of the traffic light; the app icons (Windows, macOS, Linux), the colour tray icons and the single-colour macOS menu-bar glyph are generated from it with `dev/make-icons.mjs`. The window has a header with the status pill (OK, silence, dead air, paused), softer cards and tiles, icon buttons, small transitions (off when the system asks for less motion), a light theme with more contrast, a welcome screen with the logo and settings in tabs (General, Inputs, Alerts, Updates, Backup). What the app does with the audio is unchanged.
- **Wall display: big logos.** Settings → General → Display → "Logo in the wall display": small next to the name (as before), **large** (a big column at the left of the tile) or a **soft background** behind the tile with adjustable strength. A logo with a transparent background (most PNG, SVG, WebP) no longer sits on a white box, anywhere; one that fills its picture keeps a light box so that it stays readable.
- **Wall display: clock and date as large as possible.** The time takes the top of the screen, the date under it in full; "Clock and date in the wall display" can also be Large or Normal.
- **Micro-silences.** Every drop below the threshold, however short, is now written in the event log with the time, the duration and the cause: silence (data with a low level), no data (the input stopped) or late measurements (nothing arrived for over 1.5 s: often an overloaded computer). No email or Telegram is sent for them and the moment the alarm starts does not change. Each tile counts them for today (click to see them), the dashboard shows the total, the log has a "Micro-silences" filter and the export includes them. They are kept for 30 days (Settings → General).
- **Pause the alerts of one input** (maintenance): a pause button on each tile, for 1 hour or until tomorrow 06:00. It ends by itself, so it cannot stay off by mistake and survives a restart. While paused the input is still measured and shown (PAUSED, also in the wall display) but nothing is judged, sent or sounded; the pause is written in the log; an alarm that was open ends there without a "back" message.
- **Grouped alerts.** When several inputs fail (or come back) together, the first alert goes out at once and the others within the window (60 s) follow in **one message with the list**, for the alarms and for the recoveries. Settings → Alerts: switch it off, change the window, or make the first one wait too.
- **Guided update.** The same flow as the other OnAir Garage apps: a banner with *Download and install*, *Remind me later*, *Skip this version* and the release notes; a progress bar with *Cancel*; the file is verified (SHA-256); *Close and install* (Windows starts the installer and the app quits; macOS opens the disk image; Linux shows the AppImage), after a confirmation because monitoring stops; a safety copy of the settings first (the last 3 are kept); after the update a **What's new** window and a notice. Last check and *Check now* are in Settings → Updates. Version 2 of the shared `updater` module: an interrupted or cancelled download no longer leaves a `.part` file behind.
- **Signature of the OnAir Garage apps.** A show/hide button on the email password, the Telegram token and on stream addresses that carry credentials; **name of this installation** (alerts start with `[Dead Air Watchdog · name]`, the computer name by default); Help & about with *Copy support info* (never settings, addresses or passwords), *Open logs folder* and *Check for updates*; log files per day (7 days, 5 MB each) in the `logs` folder; the window remembers its place and size.
- Tests: 178 (49 new cases: micro-silences, pause, grouped alerts, installation name, settings limits, backup keeps the machine-specific keys, updater v2, logs, support info, the icon files, logo transparency, the changelog entry).

## 26.10.5 — 2026-10-12

- **A stream that comes back turns green again.** When a stream stopped delivering data the input went to "dead air" and the alerts went out, but when the data resumed the tile and the wall display stayed red (and the recovery message could be missing) even though the meters were moving again. The web engine tells the app "stalled" when the data stops but does not say "playing" again when it resumes, and the app was waiting for that. Now the app also takes playback time moving again as proof that the audio is back. Sound cards and microphones were not affected.
- Tests: a stream that stalls and resumes (back to OK once), one that stalls and does not move (stays in fault), and the stall-then-wait-then-play case; they run the real `engine.js` against a stub of the browser audio.

## 26.10.4 — 2026-10-12

- **More than 10 streams.** With 12 streams switched on, two of them stayed "dead air", and moving them around moved the problem: the web engine inside the app opens at most 10 streams at a time (and at most 6 to the same server). The app now opens the streams in several hidden audio windows, up to 6 streams each, so all of them connect (up to 32 inputs). With 6 streams or fewer nothing changes: there is still a single window.
- **Wall display with many inputs.** Every input is now shown, from 1 up to 32: the tiles rearrange in the number of columns and rows that gives the biggest tiles, and their text scales with the tile. Before, only the first 8 fitted and the others were cut.
- **A field that fails the check is marked.** When saving is refused (for example a stream address without `http://`), the field itself turns red, its input is outlined, the message under it is red and the cursor goes to it. The same for the heartbeat address.
- Tests: how the inputs are split between the audio windows (the 12-stream case, 32 streams, the sound card, moving an input) and how the wall display picks columns and rows for 1 to 32 inputs.

## 26.10.3 — 2026-10-11

Thanks to Richard, who tried 26.10.2 on a real set of Icecast mounts and wrote to us with what did not work and what he missed: this release is made of his notes.

- **Long names in the wall display.** Mount and station names are no longer cut: they wrap on two lines and, in the new automatic mode, shrink until they fit; the same goes for the big line at the top ("DEAD AIR · name"). Settings → General → "Names in the wall display" also offers fixed sizes (large, medium, small). The dashboard tiles show the full name as a tooltip.
- **Order of the inputs.** Every input in Settings has ▲ ▼ buttons to move it up or down; the dashboard and the wall display follow the same order once you save.
- **Alerts that fail are no longer silent.** If an alert cannot be delivered (by email or Telegram) the dashboard now says so, with the reason from the server, and keeps trying as before; the notice goes away at the next delivery. Failures used to be written only in a log that the window never showed.
- **"Send test" is clearer about what is saved.** The test uses what is on the screen, while real alerts use the saved settings and only when the channel is switched on. When they differ the result now says it ("not saved yet: press Save settings", or "this channel is switched off"). If saving is refused because a field is wrong, the bar says "Not saved" and scrolls to the field.
- Tests: the whole chain from a stream going silent to the email on the wire (alarm and recovery, everybody in Bcc), the failure report, the order of the inputs and the new setting.

## 26.10.2 — 2026-10-11

- **Email to several people in one message.** The email settings take several recipients in one field (commas, spaces or new lines); one message is sent with everybody in **Bcc**, so nobody sees the other addresses. Addresses that are not valid or are repeated are left out and the app says which. The old single "To" field is converted at the first start, nothing is lost. If the server refuses an address the error names it.
- **Encrypted connection required with a user name.** With an SMTP user name the connection must be encrypted (STARTTLS, or TLS from the start): the password never travels in clear text. The email password is hidden (`***`) in error messages and in the log.
- **Telegram**: the bot token format is checked before sending and link previews are off.
- **New-version notice** by email and Telegram, once per version and per channel, switched on by default when email or Telegram is on (Settings → Updates). A channel counts as told only after the message was delivered, and what was told is kept across restarts in its own file, `alert-state.json`.
- **Configuration backup**: Settings → Configuration backup exports and imports the configuration as `dead-air-watchdog-backup-YYYY-MM-DD.json`. Passwords and tokens are left out unless you tick "Include passwords and tokens" (red warning, plain text). Import either replaces everything or adds only the inputs the computer does not have, then lists what must be typed again. The event log is not part of the backup.
- Help, README and screenshots updated; texts in English, Italian and Spanish.
- Retries of the dead-air alerts are unchanged: 30 s, 1 min, then every 2 min, for up to 30 minutes.
